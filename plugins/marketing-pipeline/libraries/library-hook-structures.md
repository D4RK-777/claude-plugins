# Library: Hook Structures
> The definitive playbook for ad hook architecture. A hook is the first 1–3 seconds of any ad (or the headline/first line of a static). It determines whether the audience stops or scrolls. Every hook in this library is a proven psychological mechanism — not a template, a trigger.

---

## HOW TO USE THIS LIBRARY

**The Hook is NOT the creative. The hook is NOT the copy.**

- **Hook** = the opening mechanism that earns attention (this document)
- **Creative Style** = the visual container (see `library-creative-types.md`)
- **Copy** = what comes after the hook earns the stop

A hook can be executed across any creative style. The same Question Hook can run in a UGC video, a static image, or a carousel card 1. The format changes — the psychological trigger doesn't.

**Hook formula:**
> `Hook Mechanism × Audience Awareness Level × Pain/Desire Proximity`

The closer the hook is to a raw nerve — specific pain, specific desire, specific identity — the higher the stop rate.

## THE 15 HOOK STRUCTURES

---

### 1. QUESTION HOOK

**What it does:** Poses a direct question the target audience is already asking themselves. Forces a micro-decision to engage.

**Psychological mechanism:** The brain is compelled to answer questions (Zeigarnik Effect). An open loop is created that demands closure.

**Best used when:** Audience is problem-aware. They know they have the problem, they just haven't found your solution.

**Awareness levels:** Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware

**Variants:**
- **Direct:** "Struggling to get leads from Meta ads?"
- **Rhetorical:** "What if you never had to cold call again?"
- **Qualifying:** "Are you still using [old/inferior method]?"
- **Empathy:** "Do you ever feel like your marketing spend just disappears?"
- **Provocative:** "Why does everyone else seem to grow faster than you?"

**Formula:** `[Pain/desire state]?` or `Why [frustrating situation] when [better alternative exists]?`

**Examples:**
- "Tired of paying for fake WhatsApp numbers?"
- "What would 10x'd open rates mean for your business?"
- "Is your ad spend actually reaching real people?"

**Pair with creative styles:** Talking Head, UGC, Pain-Problem Agitation, Education
**Pair with formats:** Short-form video, Static image (as headline)

**What to avoid:** Vague questions ("Want to grow your business?"). The question must be SPECIFIC enough that only your ICP feels addressed. If everyone can answer yes, no one feels spoken to.

---

### 2. BOLD STATEMENT / CLAIM HOOK

**What it does:** Opens with an assertive, declarative claim that is surprising, counterintuitive, or unusually specific. Creates instant credibility through confidence.

**Psychological mechanism:** Pattern interrupt + authority signalling. Certainty is attractive. Specificity signals credibility.

**Best used when:** Audience is cynical or saturated. Cut through by saying exactly what others won't.

**Awareness levels:** All levels — tune specificity to match awareness

**Variants:**
- **Outcome claim:** "We generated 847 leads in 30 days for $3/lead."
- **Contrarian claim:** "Your email open rate doesn't matter."
- **Category claim:** "This is the fastest way to validate a WhatsApp list."
- **Comparison claim:** "Our customers switched from [competitor] and cut costs by 60%."
- **Revelation:** "Most Meta ads fail before the audience even sees them."

**Formula:** `[Specific, unexpected, or bold claim] — period.`

**Examples:**
- "We 4× our ROAS by deleting 70% of our audience."
- "Bad data is silently killing your WhatsApp delivery rates."
- "The average SMS list has 34% invalid numbers. Yours probably does too."

**Pair with creative styles:** Product Hero, Social Proof, Testimonial, Us vs Them
**Pair with formats:** Static image, Short-form video, Carousel (card 1)

**What to avoid:** Vague boasts ("We're the best in the business"). Claims must be verifiable or at least plausible. False specificity erodes trust fast.

---

### 3. PROBLEM AGITATION HOOK

**What it does:** Opens directly on the pain state and makes it feel worse before offering relief. The audience feels seen and understood before they're sold anything.

**Psychological mechanism:** Empathy + pain amplification. Humans move away from pain faster than toward pleasure. Naming the pain specifically creates instant recognition ("that's me").

**Best used when:** Audience is aware of the problem but hasn't urgently addressed it. Agitation creates urgency.

**Awareness levels:** Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware

**Variants:**
- **Direct name-it:** "Sending WhatsApp campaigns to a dead list?"
- **Cost of inaction:** "Every day you run ads to invalid numbers, you're burning budget."
- **Consequence stack:** "Fake numbers → wasted spend → no ROI → campaign shut down."
- **Emotional hit:** "You're working harder than ever and your metrics are getting worse."
- **Identity threat:** "Your competitors just validated their entire list. Have you?"

**Formula:** `[Name the pain] → [Agitate the consequence] → [Leave them hanging or transition to solution]`

**Examples:**
- "You're running WhatsApp campaigns to contacts who don't exist. That's not a small problem."
- "Every invalid number in your list isn't just a wasted message — it's destroying your sender reputation."
- "Your delivery rate is tanking and you don't even know why."

**Pair with creative styles:** Pain-Problem Agitation, UGC, Talking Head, Before/After
**Pair with formats:** Short-form video, Mid-form video

**What to avoid:** Catastrophising without solution path. Audiences disengage if the pain goes too deep with no visible light. Agitate, then pivot fast.

---

### 4. SOCIAL PROOF / NUMBER HOOK

**What it does:** Opens with a credibility signal — a specific number, a customer count, a metric, a recognisable name. Borrows trust immediately.

**Psychological mechanism:** Social proof + authority. Humans defer to the wisdom of crowds. Specificity (not round numbers) signals authenticity.

**Best used when:** Audience is solution-aware or product-aware. They need a reason to choose you over alternatives.

**Awareness levels:** Solution-Aware → Product-Aware → Most Aware

**Variants:**
- **Customer count:** "Join 12,400 businesses who've already verified their lists."
- **Outcome metric:** "4,719 campaigns validated. Zero delivery complaints."
- **Testimonial stat:** "87% of our customers see a 2× lift in deliverability in week one."
- **Press/authority:** "As featured in [Publication]."
- **Growth signal:** "Grew from 0 to 50K users in 8 months."

**Formula:** `[Specific number] + [credible context]`

**Examples:**
- "Over 8 million WhatsApp numbers validated and counting."
- "Our top customer saved $23,000 in wasted ad spend last quarter."
- "4.9 stars from 600+ verified reviews."

**Pair with creative styles:** Social Proof/Press, Testimonial, Product Hero
**Pair with formats:** Static image, Carousel, Short-form video

**What to avoid:** Round numbers (they read as made up). "100,000 customers" triggers scepticism. "94,312 customers" triggers belief. Be specific.

---

### 5. CURIOSITY GAP HOOK

**What it does:** Creates an information gap between what the audience knows and what they're about to learn. Forces engagement to close the gap.

**Psychological mechanism:** The Curiosity Gap (George Loewenstein). The brain physically experiences discomfort when an open loop exists — and seeks closure. Teasing information the viewer doesn't have is one of the most reliable hooks ever tested.

**Best used when:** Audience doesn't yet know your solution exists, or doesn't know a better way is possible.

**Awareness levels:** Unaware → Problem-Aware

**Variants:**
- **The tease:** "There's a reason your WhatsApp messages aren't being read — and it's not what you think."
- **The countdown:** "3 things killing your Meta ad performance (most marketers miss #2)."
- **The reveal:** "I found out what's actually causing 34% bounce rates — watch this."
- **The secret:** "The one setting most advertisers never touch (it changes everything)."
- **The twist:** "What if your best-performing audience is the wrong audience?"

**Formula:** `[Tease outcome/insight] without [revealing the answer yet]`

**Examples:**
- "There's a silent reason your WhatsApp campaigns underperform. It takes 30 seconds to fix."
- "We ran the same ad to two audiences. One 10× outperformed the other. Here's the only difference."
- "Most businesses lose 40% of their WhatsApp ROI before a single message is sent."

**Pair with creative styles:** Education/Value-First, Demo, Founder Story, Pattern Interrupt
**Pair with formats:** Short-form video, Carousel (teases across cards)

**What to avoid:** Clickbait that under-delivers. If the "reveal" doesn't live up to the setup, you lose trust permanently. The gap must close with a genuine payoff.

---

### 6. FEAR HOOK

**What it does:** Opens by activating a specific fear — of loss, of falling behind, of making a costly mistake. Creates urgency through threat.

**Psychological mechanism:** Loss aversion (Kahneman). Humans are 2× more motivated to avoid losing something than to gain the equivalent. Fear of a negative outcome drives faster action than hope of a positive one.

**Best used when:** The cost of inaction is high and the audience hasn't felt it yet.

**Awareness levels:** Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware

**Variants:**
- **Loss of money:** "You're losing $X every time you send to an unvalidated list."
- **Loss of status:** "Your competitors are already doing this. Are you?"
- **Loss of time:** "Every month you wait costs you 3× more to fix."
- **Fear of discovery:** "What happens when your sender reputation hits zero?"
- **Fear of being wrong:** "If your current data is wrong, your entire strategy is wrong."

**Formula:** `[Threat] + [consequence of inaction]`

**Examples:**
- "WhatsApp bans accounts that push to invalid numbers. Yours might be next."
- "Your sender reputation is degrading right now — silently — with every bad send."
- "If you haven't validated your contact list, you're not running a campaign. You're running a gamble."

**Pair with creative styles:** Pain-Problem Agitation, Talking Head, Before/After
**Pair with formats:** Short-form video, Static image

**What to avoid:** Fearmongering without credibility or solution. Fear hooks that can't be backed up or relieved turn into distrust. Always have a clear solution path following the fear hook.

---

### 7. TRANSFORMATION HOOK

**What it does:** Leads with the after state — the dream outcome — and lets the audience project themselves into it. The gap between where they are and where the hook shows them creates desire.

**Psychological mechanism:** Future pacing + identity aspiration. People don't buy products, they buy who they become. The transformation hook sells the destination first.

**Best used when:** Audience aspirations are high but belief in your solution is still low.

**Awareness levels:** Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware → Product-Aware

**Variants:**
- **Outcome-first:** "Imagine sending 100,000 WhatsApp messages and knowing every one reaches a real person."
- **Identity shift:** "From spray-and-pray to surgical precision — in one step."
- **Before/After:** "[Old state] → [New state]"
- **Timeline:** "In 7 days, your delivery rates could look like this."
- **Social:** "This is what [customer type] looks like after 30 days with [product]."

**Formula:** `[Dream outcome or after state] + [timeframe or simple path]`

**Examples:**
- "Clean data. Zero waste. Maximum ROI. That's what 90 days with GreenTier looks like."
- "From 40% bounce rate to 2% — without changing your campaign strategy."
- "What if every dollar of ad spend actually reached a real, active contact?"

**Pair with creative styles:** Before/After, Lifestyle, Testimonial, Founder Story
**Pair with formats:** Static image, Short-form video, Mid-form video

**What to avoid:** Over-promising. The transformation must be believable and achievable. Inflated claims ("become a millionaire overnight") destroy trust even if technically legal.

---

### 8. CONTRARIAN / MYTH-BUST HOOK

**What it does:** Challenges a widely-held belief in the audience's industry or niche. Creates cognitive dissonance that demands resolution.

**Psychological mechanism:** Cognitive dissonance + intellectual curiosity. When a statement contradicts something the viewer believes, they must engage to resolve the conflict. Contrarianism also signals expertise ("they know something others don't").

**Best used when:** The market is noisy and undifferentiated. Standing against the consensus is the fastest way to stand out.

**Awareness levels:** All levels — especially effective for saturated markets

**Variants:**
- **Direct myth-bust:** "[Common belief] is wrong. Here's what actually works."
- **Unpopular opinion:** "Hot take: your email list is worth less than your WhatsApp list right now."
- **Industry callout:** "The marketing advice you've been following is designed for brands 10× your size."
- **Data-backed contrarian:** "We tested this 400 times. The 'best practice' loses every time."
- **The inversion:** "Stop optimising your ads. Start optimising your list."

**Formula:** `[Common belief] is [wrong/outdated/a lie] + [real insight]`

**Examples:**
- "More leads isn't your problem. Bad leads are your problem."
- "Open rates are a vanity metric. Delivery rates are what actually matter."
- "Most 'data-driven' campaigns are built on garbage data. Here's why that matters."

**Pair with creative styles:** Us vs Them, Talking Head, Education/Value-First, Founder Story
**Pair with formats:** Short-form video, Carousel, Static image

**What to avoid:** Contrarianism for shock value. If you can't back it up with a credible argument or data, you lose authority fast. The hook must lead somewhere true.

---

### 9. STORY HOOK

**What it does:** Opens with a narrative fragment — a specific moment, character, or turning point — that pulls the viewer into a story they need to see resolved.

**Psychological mechanism:** Narrative transportation. Humans are wired for story. Once inside a narrative, resistance drops. The viewer becomes a participant, not a target.

**Best used when:** Building emotional depth. Products with a compelling origin story, transformation case study, or vivid customer journey.

**Awareness levels:** Unaware → Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware

**Variants:**
- **In medias res (start in the middle):** "We almost lost everything. Here's what saved us."
- **Character-first:** "Three years ago, [customer name] was sending campaigns to half a million dead numbers."
- **Moment-specific:** "On a Tuesday morning in March, I noticed something wrong with our delivery data."
- **Setup/conflict:** "I built this product because I couldn't find it anywhere else."
- **Customer journey:** "She'd tried everything. Then she ran a single validation and never looked back."

**Formula:** `[Specific moment] + [Character in conflict] + [open loop to resolution]`

**Examples:**
- "We lost $47,000 before we found out our list was 60% invalid numbers."
- "His campaign had a 94% delivery rate. I wanted to know exactly what he was doing differently."
- "I used to think data hygiene was for enterprise brands. Then we scaled."

**Pair with creative styles:** Founder Story, UGC, Testimonial, Lifestyle
**Pair with formats:** Mid-form video, Long-form video, Carousel (multi-card story arc)

**What to avoid:** Too slow. Story hooks must earn attention fast. The character, conflict, and stakes must all be established within 5 seconds or the viewer exits. No slow-burn setups for paid social.

---

### 10. US VS THEM HOOK

**What it does:** Creates a clear in-group identity ("people like us") versus an out-group or old way. Audience self-selects into the in-group.

**Psychological mechanism:** Tribal identity + status differentiation. Belonging to the winning side is a powerful motivator. This hook works on self-image: "I'm not one of those people who does things the old/dumb way."

**Best used when:** Competitors are clearly distinguishable or a before/after mental model makes sense.

**Awareness levels:** Solution-Aware → Product-Aware

**Variants:**
- **Smart vs naive:** "Smart marketers validate first. They don't guess."
- **Winners vs losers:** "Brands scaling in 2025 all have one thing in common."
- **Old way vs new way:** "The old way: send to everyone, hope for the best. The new way: validate, then send."
- **In-group signalling:** "If you care about data quality, this was built for you."
- **Competitor contrast:** "[Competitor] charges 3× more for half the accuracy."

**Formula:** `[Them/old way] vs [Us/new way] — the contrast speaks for itself`

**Examples:**
- "There are two types of WhatsApp marketers: those who validate their lists and those who wonder why campaigns fail."
- "Most tools tell you what to send. We tell you who's actually there to receive it."
- "Old approach: buy a list, blast it, pray. New approach: validate first, spend less, convert more."

**Pair with creative styles:** Us vs Them/Comparison, Testimonial, Education/Value-First
**Pair with formats:** Static image, Carousel, Short-form video

**What to avoid:** Punching down. Insulting non-customers or competitors breeds defensiveness, not conversion. The hook should make the viewer want to be in the winning camp — not feel attacked for being in the losing one.

---

### 11. FOUNDER CONFESSION HOOK

**What it does:** Opens with a personal admission of failure, mistake, or hard lesson — from the founder or a trusted figure inside the brand. Radical vulnerability that builds trust.

**Psychological mechanism:** Authenticity + parasocial connection. In a world of polish and spin, an honest confession cuts through as genuinely human. Imperfection signals trustworthiness. Vulnerability creates a felt connection.

**Best used when:** B2B audiences, high-consideration products, trust is the main purchase barrier.

**Awareness levels:** Unaware → Problem-Aware

**Variants:**
- **Mistake confession:** "I made this product because I made a catastrophically expensive mistake."
- **Learning confession:** "It took me three failed campaigns to understand what actually matters."
- **Belief-reversal:** "I used to think data quality was boring. I was wrong."
- **Humble brag (reverse):** "We almost shut down the company. Here's what we learned."
- **Transparency:** "I'll be honest — the first version of this was terrible."

**Formula:** `[Personal failure/mistake] + [what it cost/taught] + [what changed]`

**Examples:**
- "I wasted $180,000 on Meta ads before I realised our list was full of fake numbers."
- "When we launched, we had one customer and zero confidence. Here's where we are now."
- "I built this tool because I was the target customer — and I was furious at every alternative."

**Pair with creative styles:** Founder Story, Talking Head, UGC
**Pair with formats:** Mid-form video, Long-form video (needs time to build)

**What to avoid:** Confessions that seem calculated or performative. If it doesn't feel genuine, it reads as a manipulation tactic. Real founders, real stories, real imperfections.

---

### 12. RELATABLE MOMENT HOOK

**What it does:** Opens with a hyper-specific, recognisable moment from the target audience's daily experience. Creates instant "that's me" identification.

**Psychological mechanism:** Recognition + empathy. When an ad mirrors your exact reality back at you, defences drop. You feel understood, not sold to. Specificity is the unlock — generic relatable moments don't work; specific ones do.

**Best used when:** You know the daily reality of your ICP intimately. The more specific and niche the moment, the higher the identification.

**Awareness levels:** Problem-Aware (especially powerful here)

**Variants:**
- **Daily frustration:** "When your WhatsApp campaign gets 0 replies and you have no idea why."
- **The exact moment:** "You're about to hit send on a 200K list and you realise you have no idea how old that data is."
- **Professional reality:** "The monthly report meeting where someone asks why deliverability dropped."
- **Internal monologue:** "Me, staring at a 22% open rate like it means anything."
- **Peer comparison:** "When your competitor's campaign goes viral and yours got 0 replies."

**Formula:** `[Specific, recognisable scene or feeling from ICP daily life]`

**Examples:**
- "That feeling when you've spent $2,000 on ads and WhatsApp shows 0 delivered messages."
- "Your operations team asking why the campaign data doesn't match the CRM. Again."
- "Sending 50,000 WhatsApp messages and watching your reply rate flatline in real time."

**Pair with creative styles:** UGC, Meme/Cultural, Talking Head, Pain-Problem Agitation
**Pair with formats:** Short-form video, Static image

**What to avoid:** Generic relatable moments ("We all know how hard marketing is"). They feel hollow. The power comes from a moment so specific it feels like you've been watching their screen.

---

### 13. URGENCY / SCARCITY HOOK

**What it does:** Creates immediate pressure to act by limiting time, quantity, or access. Converts browsers into buyers by making inaction costly.

**Psychological mechanism:** Loss aversion + FOMO. The fear of missing out on something available now triggers action faster than desire for gain. Temporal or quantity constraints override the "I'll think about it" response.

**Best used when:** Retargeting warm audiences who've already seen your product but haven't converted. Bottom-of-funnel.

**Awareness levels:** Product-Aware → Most Aware

**Variants:**
- **Time-based:** "Offer ends Friday. After that, standard pricing."
- **Quantity-based:** "Only 50 validation credits left at this price."
- **Access-based:** "This feature is currently in private beta. 47 spots left."
- **Event-triggered:** "Launching in 3 days. Early adopters get lifetime pricing."
- **Social urgency:** "800 businesses validated their lists this week. Don't be the last."

**Formula:** `[Specific limit] + [what they'll lose if they don't act]`

**Examples:**
- "We're closing this pricing tier at midnight on Friday."
- "Last 100 spots in the GreenTier validation batch — after that, wait 2 weeks."
- "Campaign season is 6 weeks away. If your list isn't validated by then, you're behind."

**Pair with creative styles:** Product Hero, Social Proof, Offer/Urgency formats
**Pair with formats:** Static image, Short-form video, Carousel

**What to avoid:** False scarcity. If the offer never actually expires or the supply never actually runs out, audiences learn to ignore your urgency signals — permanently. Credibility is worth more than a single conversion.

---

### 14. EDUCATION / VALUE-FIRST HOOK

**What it does:** Opens by immediately delivering genuine value — a tip, a data point, a framework, an insight. Earns attention by being useful before being promotional.

**Psychological mechanism:** Reciprocity (Cialdini). When you receive value, you feel compelled to reciprocate. An ad that teaches you something useful builds goodwill debt that converts downstream.

**Best used when:** Trust is the main barrier. Complex products, B2B audiences, sceptical markets. Also powerful for cold audience awareness.

**Awareness levels:** Unaware → Problem-Aware

**Variants:**
- **Tip-first:** "3 reasons your WhatsApp delivery rate drops below 70%."
- **Data hook:** "The average B2B contact list decays by 22% every 12 months."
- **Framework:** "How to think about data quality in 3 layers: [capture → validate → maintain]."
- **Fact reveal:** "Most businesses think deliverability is a campaign problem. It's actually a data problem."
- **How-to open:** "Here's the fastest way to check if your WhatsApp list is clean."

**Formula:** `[Specific insight, tip, or data] that the viewer didn't know before`

**Examples:**
- "Quick data hygiene check: pull your last 3 WhatsApp campaigns and calculate actual delivery rate. If it's under 85%, your list has a problem."
- "What nobody tells you about WhatsApp Business API delivery rates and why yours is probably lower than you think."
- "The 4 types of invalid WhatsApp numbers — and why only one of them actually damages your sender score."

**Pair with creative styles:** Education/Value-First, Carousel (tip-by-tip), Talking Head
**Pair with formats:** Carousel, Short-form video, Mid-form video

**What to avoid:** Watered-down tips. If the value is generic enough to apply to anyone, it applies to no one. The more specific and actionable the education, the more trust it builds. Vague tips insult intelligent audiences.

---

### 15. PATTERN INTERRUPT HOOK

**What it does:** Does something so unexpected in the first frame or first line that the viewer physically pauses their scroll to process what they just saw. Context-breaking, tonally jarring, or visually surprising.

**Psychological mechanism:** Orienting response. The brain is hardwired to respond to novelty and incongruity. Something unexpected activates the "what is this?" reflex before conscious decision-making kicks in. You can't help but stop.

**Best used when:** Heavy ad saturation in your vertical. When audiences are numb to standard ads. Top-of-funnel, cold audiences.

**Awareness levels:** Unaware → Problem-Aware

**Variants:**
- **Visual interrupt:** Unexpected colour, unexpected person, unexpected environment
- **Tonal interrupt:** Starting with silence, or with a completely off-topic statement
- **Statement interrupt:** "This is not a sponsored post." / "I'm going to say something controversial."
- **Format interrupt:** Text only in a video feed. Audio only. Reversed expectations.
- **Emotional interrupt:** Genuine laughter or genuine shock in first 1 second
- **Direct address:** Breaking the fourth wall — "Yes, I'm talking to you."

**Formula:** `[Something the viewer's brain cannot auto-process] → [hook into message]`

**Examples:**
- "Stop. Before you scroll past this: do you know what percentage of your WhatsApp contacts are fake?"
- Opening frame: blank white screen, single text line: "Your list is broken."
- "Okay wait — I need to say something that might make you uncomfortable."
- Starting mid-sentence: "...and THAT is why we lost $40,000 in one campaign."

**Pair with creative styles:** Pattern Interrupt, Meme/Cultural, UGC
**Pair with formats:** Short-form video (first frame is everything), Static image

**What to avoid:** Interrupting without earning trust immediately after. The pattern interrupt buys you 3–5 seconds of attention. If the hook that follows is weak, you've wasted the disruption. Interrupt, then immediately deliver on the attention.

## HOOK TESTING FRAMEWORK

**Test one hook variable at a time:**

1. **Hook Mechanism** — which psychological trigger? (First priority to test)
2. **Hook Specificity** — vague vs specific version of the same mechanism
3. **Hook Person** — who delivers it? (Founder, customer, actor, voiceover)
4. **Hook Format** — visual vs text vs audio-first

**Hook quality scoring (before testing):**
- [ ] Does it speak to a specific audience? (not everyone)
- [ ] Does it open a loop that needs closing?
- [ ] Is it specific enough to be credible?
- [ ] Does it earn 3+ more seconds of attention?
- [ ] Would you watch/read past it yourself?

**Retirement signal:** CTR drops 30% week-over-week. Frequency exceeds 3.0. Rotate.

## Mode C — Where-to-Start Diagnostic (run this FIRST if anyone is new)

Don't dump a checklist. Ask three small clusters, one at a time, and adapt.

**Cluster 1 — The Business:**
1. What are you selling, and what does it cost?
2. Who has bought it so far (if anyone), and why?
3. What's your monthly ad budget?

**Cluster 2 — The Asset Inventory:**
4. Do you have a landing page or website the ads can send traffic to?
5. Do you have any creative assets (video, images, customer testimonials, UGC)?
6. Are your ad accounts and pixel/CAPI set up?

**Cluster 3 — The Constraint:**
7. What does winning look like in 7 days? (sign-ups, sales, demos, leads)
8. What's the one thing you're afraid will go wrong?

Synthesize this into a one-paragraph **Sprint Readiness Brief** that scores them on:
- **Offer clarity** (do they know what they sell?)
- **Funnel completeness** (can they receive traffic?)
- **Creative readiness** (do they have raw material for ads?)
- **Budget realism** (can the budget produce a statistically meaningful test?)
- **Tracking integrity** (will they know what worked?)

Then route them: **GREEN** → proceed to 7-Day Sprint. **YELLOW** → 1-day fix-up, then sprint. **RED** → they're not ready for ads yet, point them to `first-customer-finder` or `positioning-engine`.

### Minimum Viable Budget Reality Check

A statistical truth most beginners miss: **you cannot meaningfully test on $5/day.** The math:

- Each ad needs ~50 conversions to know if it's a winner with any confidence
- If your target CPA is $20, that's $1,000 per ad to validate
- Most tests run 3-5 ad variants
- **Floor for a legitimate Meta test: $50-100/day for ~7 days = $350-700**
- **Floor for Google Search: $30-50/day for branded + non-branded** (Search converts faster with smaller data)
- **Floor for TikTok / YouTube: $100/day** (creative-driven, needs reach for the algo to find pockets)

Below these floors, recommend they invest in **content + organic + cold outreach** until they have $1,500+ to deploy. Tell them straight. Don't pretend $200 will produce a scaled campaign.

## Mode A — The 7-Day Conversion Sprint

This is the primary deliverable. The plan that turns "I want customers" into "I have customers" in 7 days. Adapt the cadence to weekdays/weekends as needed; the day numbers are sequence, not literal calendar.

### Day 1 — Offer & Foundation

The single highest-leverage activity in paid acquisition is **fixing the offer before spending a dollar**. A great offer overcomes a mediocre ad. A great ad cannot save a weak offer.

**Run the Hormozi Value Equation on their current offer:**

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
          (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

Walk through each lever:
- **Dream Outcome** — Is the outcome they're selling visceral and specific? "Save 10 hours a week" beats "improve efficiency."
- **Perceived Likelihood** — Where's the proof? Testimonials, guarantees, case studies, before/afters, social proof?
- **Time Delay** — How fast does the prospect see results? Can you compress it? Quick wins in the first 24-48h destroy abandonment.
- **Effort & Sacrifice** — What's the prospect's job? Can you do more of it for them? "Done-for-you" outperforms "DIY" 3-5x at the same price.

Then construct a **Grand Slam Offer** using the four stack components:
1. **Core deliverable** (the product itself)
2. **Bonus stack** (3-5 bonuses, each addressing a specific objection: "what if I don't have time", "what if it doesn't work", "what if I get stuck")
3. **Guarantee** (the stronger and weirder, the better — "Pay only if it works", "Double your money back if you don't get X in 30 days")
4. **Urgency/Scarcity** (real, not fake — limited spots, limited time, limited bonus)

**Deliverable for Day 1:** A written offer document the user can paste into a landing page. See `references/offer-design-vault.md` for the full Grand Slam Offer construction process, bonus templates, and guarantee formulas.

**Also on Day 1 — fix tracking:**
- Meta Pixel + Conversions API (server-side) installed and firing
- Google Tag (gtag) + GA4 conversions
- UTM parameters defined for every campaign (`utm_source / utm_medium / utm_campaign / utm_content / utm_term`)
- A spreadsheet for daily KPIs (see `references/funnel-math-and-tracking.md`)

If tracking is broken, **everything else is wishful thinking.** Do not skip this.

### Day 2 — Audience & Big Idea

**The Big Idea** (Todd Brown) is the singular insight your ads will lead with. Not your product. Not your feature. The *worldview shift* that makes your offer feel inevitable.

Examples:
- Hims didn't sell hair-loss pills. They sold "*hair loss is medical, not shameful*."
- AG1 didn't sell greens powder. They sold "*one drink replaces 10 supplements*."
- Liquid Death didn't sell water. They sold "*murder your thirst*."

Generate **3 candidate Big Ideas** for the user's offer. For each, write the one-sentence worldview shift, the enemy/villain it positions against, and the emotional payoff.

**Audience mapping using Schwartz's 5 Awareness Levels:**

| Level | They know... | Lead with... |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Unaware | Nothing about the problem | A story, a shocking stat, a curiosity hook |
| 2. Problem-Aware | They have the problem | The problem + your unique mechanism |
| 3. Solution-Aware | Solutions exist | Why your solution is different (mechanism) |
| 4. Product-Aware | They know your product | Differentiation + proof + offer |
| 5. Most Aware | They want it | Price, offer, urgency |

**For each audience segment, decide:**
- Where do they hang out? (specific subreddits, Twitter/X accounts, podcasts, YouTube channels)
- What words do *they* use for the problem? (not your words — theirs; mine reviews, forums)
- What have they already tried? (your ad must acknowledge this without naming competitors directly)
- What objections will fire automatically? (price, trust, time, "will this work for me")

**Deliverable for Day 2:** A Big Idea + 3 audience segments, each with the lead-in hook, the words they use, and the top 3 objections. This becomes the creative brief.

### Day 3 — Creative Production

The single biggest lever in modern paid media is **creative volume + diversity**. Meta, TikTok, and YouTube algorithms reward creative iteration. One brilliant ad is worth less than 5 different ad concepts you can rotate.

**The 5-Concept Creative Sprint** (produce all 5 — diversity is the unlock):

1. **Founder POV / Origin Story** — face-to-camera, why this exists, what changed. Highest authenticity, often highest CTR.
2. **Problem-Agitation-Solution (PAS)** — name the pain in their language, twist the knife, reveal the solution.
3. **Listicle / "X Ways to Y"** — "5 mistakes that kill X" or "3 reasons your Y is failing." Scrollable, educational, trojan-horse pitch.
4. **Social Proof Compilation** — UGC, testimonials, before/after, screenshots of results. The strongest format if you have proof.
5. **Pattern Interrupt / Bold Statement** — controversial take, opposite-of-conventional-wisdom, "stop doing X." Hook-driven.

**Every ad needs three layers** (Shackelford's framework):

1. **Hook** (first 3 seconds / first 5 words) — stops the scroll. See the Hook Library below.
2. **Body** — delivers the value proposition, builds belief, handles objections.
3. **CTA** — tells them exactly what to do and why now. ("Get the kit before Friday" beats "Learn more".)

**The Hook Library — 12 proven openers:**

1. *"I tried X for 30 days. Here's what happened."* (Story curiosity)
2. *"Stop doing [common practice]. Here's why."* (Pattern interrupt)
3. *"If you [specific symptom], you're probably [insight]."* (Diagnostic hook)
4. *"The reason [outcome] isn't happening for you is..."* (Mechanism hook)
5. *"How [specific person] went from [before] to [after] in [time]."* (Transformation)
6. *"3 mistakes I made before I figured out [outcome]."* (Confessional listicle)
7. *"Most people think [X]. The truth is [Y]."* (Contrarian)
8. *"This is the [thing] I wish I'd had 5 years ago."* (Endorsement)
9. *"You don't have a [surface problem]. You have a [real problem]."* (Reframe)
10. *"Here's exactly what [achievement] looks like, step by step."* (Demonstration)
11. *"$[Number] later, here's what I learned about [topic]."* (Cost-of-experience)
12. *"Read this before you [common action]."* (Warning hook)

For deeper hook construction, copy formulas, and script templates, see `references/creative-and-copy-formulas.md`.

**Production minimums:**
- 5 ad concepts × 2-3 variations each = 10-15 ads ready to test
- Each concept in **both** 9:16 (Reels/Stories/TikTok) and 1:1 or 4:5 (feed)
- Use existing UGC, screen recordings, founder selfie videos — **do not** delay launch for a polished shoot. Iteration speed beats production quality 9 times out of 10.

### Day 4 — Funnel & Campaign Build

**Pick ONE primary platform.** Beginners spread budget across Meta + Google + TikTok and learn nothing on any of them. Concentrate.

**Platform decision tree:**

- **Visual / impulse / discovery product (eCom, DTC, consumer apps)** → Meta first (Advantage+ Shopping for eCom, Sales objective for app/lead gen)
- **Search-intent / high-AOV B2B / known-need ($1K+ AOV, "how do I solve X")** → Google Search first (then PMax)
- **Younger demo, entertainment-adjacent, creator-led** → TikTok first
- **B2B SaaS / professional services / decision-makers** → LinkedIn for ICP precision, Google for intent capture, Meta for retargeting
- **High-AOV considered purchase with explanation needs** → YouTube (long-form VSL) + Google Search

Platform-specific campaign builds: see `references/platform-playbooks.md` — exhaustive Meta, Google, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, X playbooks with structure, naming conventions, and bid strategies.

**Day 4 build checklist:**
- 1 primary platform chosen
- 1 campaign objective (Sales/Conversions for most, Leads for B2B)
- 1-3 ad sets (don't over-segment audiences day one — let the algorithm work)
- 10-15 ads loaded
- UTMs on every link
- Conversion event chosen (Purchase, Lead, Sign-up — never just "Page View" for paid)
- Daily budget set: enough for 3-5 conversions per day at target CPA, or kill the test before it begins
- Conversions API verified firing

### Day 5 — Launch + First 24 Hours

**Launch in the morning** (your buyer's morning, not yours). The algorithm uses the first 24h to find pockets — give it as much daytime as possible.

**The First 24h Rules:**
- Do NOT pause anything for at least 24 hours. The learning phase needs data.
- Do NOT make budget changes >20% in either direction (kicks campaigns back into learning).
- Monitor for **catastrophic** issues only: ads disapproved, pixel not firing, landing page 404. Otherwise — leave it alone.
- Watch hook rate (3s video view rate) and CTR as early signals. Conversion data takes 24-72h to be meaningful.

**Hour 0-24 healthy ranges (rough benchmarks — vary by industry):**
- Meta CTR (link click): 1-3% (under 1% = creative is broken, over 4% = check for click fraud or wrong audience)
- Meta hook rate (3s view rate): 25-45% (under 20% = hook is weak)
- Google Search CTR: 5-10% on brand, 2-5% on non-brand
- TikTok CTR: 1-2% (and TT users click less but watch more — judge by view-through purchases too)

### Day 6 — Read the Signals, Make the Cuts

The **48-72h decision protocol**:

| Spend at target CPA | Outcome | Action |
|---|---|---|
| <0.5× target CPA spent, 0 conversions | Not enough data | Wait |
| 1× target CPA spent, 0 conversions | Concerning | Check tracking; if clean, keep watching |
| 2× target CPA spent, 0 conversions | **KILL** | Pause ad/ad set, diagnose |
| 3× target CPA spent, 1 conversion | Borderline | Pause if hook rate also weak; otherwise watch |
| Spent target CPA × 1, got 1+ conversions at or below target | **WINNER** | Note it, don't touch it |

**Diagnostic when nothing converts:**
1. Is the click-through rate weak? → Hook/creative problem. New angles needed.
2. Is the CTR strong but CPA bad? → Landing page or offer problem. Audit with `website-conversion-analyst`.
3. Is hook rate good but CTR weak? → CTA or middle of ad is weak.
4. Is everything good except final purchase? → Checkout friction, price objection, missing trust signals.

### Day 7 — Iterate, Plan Week 2

**The Iteration Triad:**
1. **Kill** anything 2× target CPA with no conversions.
2. **Duplicate** winners into a new ad set with a slightly higher budget (20-50% increase, not 200%). Sudden budget shocks reset learning.
3. **Brief Week 2 creative** based on what won: which angle, which hook, which proof point. Make 5 new variations of the winning concept.

**Plan Week 2:**
- Layer retargeting if you have warm traffic (website visitors, video viewers, engagers)
- Build a lookalike if you've crossed ~50 conversions on Meta
- Add a second creative concept family (don't over-pivot, expand)
- Stress-test the offer further: try a stronger guarantee, a bigger bonus, a tighter scarcity mechanism

**Deliverable for Day 7:** A one-page Week-2 plan covering: what survived, what's new, what's the budget, what's the target.

## Mode D — Triage & Fix (operational sub-mode)

When the user shows up with an active problem ("my ROAS dropped from 3 to 1", "ads stopped converting", "CAC is killing me"), skip the sprint and go straight to diagnosis.

**The Triage Tree:**

1. **Was anything changed in the last 72 hours?** (new creative, budget shock, audience change, landing page edit, pixel/tracking change). Most "sudden" drops are self-inflicted.
2. **Is tracking still working?** (iOS 17/18 updates, ad blockers, CAPI lag — verify pixel + server-side parity).
3. **Has creative fatigued?** (frequency >3 on Meta, CTR dropping 20%+ over 7 days, CPM rising)
4. **Has the audience saturated?** (Was a small lookalike, now exhausted?)
5. **Is the offer still differentiated?** (Did a competitor copy or undercut?)
6. **Are macro forces in play?** (seasonality, holiday CPM spikes, election cycles, news events)

For each diagnosis, prescribe the specific fix — see `references/platform-playbooks.md` for platform-specific recovery moves.

## The Five Numbers That Matter

Every conversation about paid acquisition should anchor in these five. If the user doesn't know them, get them. If they're wrong, fix them before doing anything else.

1. **CAC** (Customer Acquisition Cost) — total spend ÷ customers acquired. Includes ad spend, agency fees, creative production.
2. **LTV** (Lifetime Value) — total gross profit a customer generates over their lifetime (use 12-month or 24-month LTV for early stage — true LTV is unknowable).
3. **Payback period** — months to recover CAC from gross profit. <12 months for venture-scale SaaS, <3 months for eCom, <6 months for most service businesses.
4. **MER** (Marketing Efficiency Ratio) = total revenue ÷ total ad spend. The blended truth-teller that survives attribution shenanigans. Aim for industry benchmarks: eCom 3-5x, SaaS B2B 1.5-3x.
5. **Contribution Margin** — revenue minus all variable costs (COGS, payment processing, shipping, ad spend). The number that decides if you can scale or you're paying for growth.

Full breakdown, formulas, and tracking templates: `references/funnel-math-and-tracking.md`.

## Operating Principles

These are non-negotiable. If the user pushes you to skip them, hold the line.

1. **Tracking before traffic.** Never recommend launching a campaign with broken or unverified tracking. Bad data is worse than no data — it makes you confidently wrong.
2. **Offer before creative.** A great ad on a weak offer is just expensive education for the prospect.
3. **One platform at a time, for beginners.** Mastery on one channel beats dabbling on three.
4. **Statistical patience.** Most "this isn't working" calls happen before the data is meaningful. Trust the volume thresholds.
5. **Kill quickly, scale slowly.** Asymmetry: killing a loser costs you nothing; killing a winner costs you everything. Scale in 20-30% increments.
6. **Creative is the variable; algorithms find the audience.** In 2025+ media buying, you don't out-target the algorithm. You out-creative the competition.
7. **Be the friend who tells the truth.** If the budget is too small, say it. If the offer is weak, say it. If they're not ready for ads, say it. Direct-response respect.
8. **Always end with the next action.** Every output finishes with "**Do this next:**" and one specific, time-bounded move.

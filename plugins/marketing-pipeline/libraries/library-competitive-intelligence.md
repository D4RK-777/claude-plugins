# Library: Competitive Intelligence
> Frameworks for READING the market — not writing into it. This library is used during the research phase only. Analysis produces structured intelligence. Creative execution uses that intelligence. These are separate operations — never conflate them.

---

## THE RESEARCH DISCIPLINE

**The single most important rule in this library:**

> Research = raw collection with zero interpretation.
> Analysis = pattern extraction from raw data, with zero new data gathering.
> Strategy = synthesis of analysis into directional decisions.
> Creative = execution of strategy.

Running these simultaneously degrades all four. A researcher who analyses while gathering will bias their collection toward confirming what they've already half-concluded. An analyst who gathers while pattern-matching will stop when they find what they're looking for rather than when collection is complete.

**Run them as sequential phases. Gate each phase before the next begins.**

## PART 1: THE 6 COMPETITIVE COPY PATTERNS

Every paid ad in existence uses one of these six opening mechanisms. When you read competitor ads, classify each one. The distribution tells you what's saturated, what's absent, and where to be different.

---

### Pattern 1: STAT HOOK
**Structure:** Specific number with unit of measurement
**Reads as:** Data-confident, credibility-first, rational appeal
**Example form:** "[X]% [outcome]. [Y]% [metric]."

**What it signals about the competitor:**
They believe their audience is analytical and responds to proof before story. They have metrics they're confident in and are willing to lead with them. Implies they're mid-to-bottom funnel focus.

**When it's saturated:** Everyone in the category is leading with numbers → go empathy or story
**When it's absent:** The category is long on claims and short on proof → Stat Hook becomes the differentiator

**Analysis prompt:** What numbers are competitors citing? Are they outcomes (conversion rate) or vanity metrics (users, reviews)? Outcome metrics convert. Vanity metrics are commodity claims.

---

### Pattern 2: PAIN HOOK
**Structure:** Problem → consequence → (implied or stated) solution
**Reads as:** Empathetic, problem-aware, knows the audience's reality
**Example form:** "[Frustrating situation]? [Consequence they fear]. [Relief exists]."

**What it signals about the competitor:**
They've done VOC research or they're close to the customer. They're targeting problem-aware audiences who know they have the issue but haven't urgently addressed it.

**When it's saturated:** Every competitor opens on the same pain → find a pain they're ignoring, or reframe the same pain from a new angle
**When it's absent:** Surprisingly common gap — category is heavy on product features and light on customer empathy → Pain Hook creates instant differentiation through recognition

**Analysis prompt:** Which specific pains are competitors naming? Which pains are real for customers that competitors aren't touching? The unaddressed pains are the whitespace.

---

### Pattern 3: CASE STUDY HOOK
**Structure:** Named brand/customer + specific measurable outcome
**Reads as:** Authority through association, proof through example, outcome-first
**Example form:** "[Named company] achieved [specific metric] using [product/approach]."

**What it signals about the competitor:**
They have notable customers they can name and are using them as social currency. Signals established market presence. Effective for B2B where social proof from recognisable names carries weight.

**When it's saturated:** Everyone is name-dropping their best-known clients → go granular (find the relatable mid-market case study rather than the enterprise one)
**When it's absent:** The category is abstract and theoretical → Case Study Hook grounds the conversation in reality

**Analysis prompt:** Whose customers are competitors citing? What outcomes are they highlighting? Are these aspirational customers (big brands) or relatable ones (same size as your ICP)? Your ICP needs to see themselves in the case study.

---

### Pattern 4: SOCIAL PROOF HOOK
**Structure:** Volume number + authority signal (partner badge, rating, platform recognition)
**Reads as:** Safety in numbers, institutional legitimacy, reduced purchase risk
**Example form:** "[X] businesses / [Y] stars / [Z] certified partner."

**What it signals about the competitor:**
They're prioritising trust and risk-reduction over aspiration. Usually mid-to-bottom funnel. Often the default move when a product is complex or expensive and purchase anxiety is real.

**When it's saturated:** Everyone is citing customer counts and ratings → these become table stakes, not differentiators
**When it's absent:** The category is emerging or unproven → Social Proof Hook reduces category scepticism, not just brand scepticism

**Analysis prompt:** What proof signals are competitors using? Star ratings, install counts, partnership badges, certifications? Which of those signals does your brand have that isn't being used? Which doesn't your brand have yet?

---

### Pattern 5: PRICING HOOK
**Structure:** Discount, free tier, price comparison, or cost-of-alternatives
**Reads as:** Price-sensitive audience, acquisition-focused, low barrier to trial
**Example form:** "[X]% off / Free until [milestone] / Costs less than [alternative]."

**What it signals about the competitor:**
They're competing on price or acquisition friction. Either they're in a race to the bottom, or they're using pricing as a trial mechanism to build a funnel from which they upsell. Both have implications for positioning.

**When it's saturated:** Category has become a price war → exit the conversation entirely, compete on outcome value not price point
**When it's absent:** The category is premium-positioned → Pricing Hook can disrupt if you have genuine price advantage

**Analysis prompt:** At what price points are competitors advertising? Is their pricing hook a genuine value proposition or a temporary acquisition play? If everyone is leading with price, the category is commoditising — and you need to lead with outcome.

---

### Pattern 6: EMPATHY HOOK
**Structure:** Emotional acknowledgment of the audience's state before any product mention
**Reads as:** Deeply human, audience-first, emotionally intelligent
**Example form:** "What if [dream state]?" or "Imagine [aspirational outcome] without [current pain]."

**What it signals about the competitor:**
They're targeting emotionally — aspiration, identity, or relief from stress. Often top-of-funnel, often for products where the emotional job-to-be-done is as important as the functional one.

**When it's saturated:** Category is full of "imagine if" aspirational ads → go concrete (data, proof, specifics)
**When it's absent:** The category is transactional and dry → Empathy Hook creates unexpectedly strong cut-through

**Analysis prompt:** What emotion are competitors evoking? Relief? Ambition? Belonging? Fear? The emotion they're not evoking is often the one your ICP is actually feeling.

## PART 2: THE TOV SPECTRUM

Map every competitor to a position on this axis. Then find where your brand sits and where the white space is.

```
Aggressive ← → Data-Driven ← → Professional ← → Price-First ← → Practical ← → Aspirational ← → Empathetic
```

**How to score:**
- Read 10+ ads per competitor
- Identify the dominant emotional register (not the subject, the register)
- Place them on the spectrum based on the majority of their copy

**What to record per competitor:**
```
COMPETITOR: [Name]
TOV Position: [Spectrum point]
Key characteristic: [1 sentence — how they sound]
Ad copy example: [Direct quote from an actual ad]
```

**Strategic use of the spectrum:**
- If most competitors cluster left (aggressive, data-driven) → move right (aspirational, empathetic)
- If most competitors cluster right (aspirational, empathetic) → move left (specific, data-driven, direct)
- If they're evenly distributed → find the one position with the most cultural tension and own it
- Your brand does NOT have to occupy the "best" position — it has to occupy the most defensible UNCROWDED position

## PART 3: CREATIVE FORMAT DISTRIBUTION

For each competitor, record what percentage of their active ads use each format. Do not analyse — just count.

```
COMPETITOR: [Name]
Total active ads observed: [N]
Format breakdown:
  - Static image: [%]
  - Short-form video (0-15s): [%]
  - Mid-form video (15-60s): [%]
  - Carousel: [%]
  - Collection: [%]

Dominant creative style (see library-creative-types.md):
  - Most common style: [e.g. UGC Talking Head]
  - Second most common: [e.g. Demo / Screen Recording]
  - Absent styles: [what they're not doing]
```

**Strategic use:**
Absent formats + absent styles = your creative opportunity. If every competitor is running mid-form demo videos, a well-crafted static image with a Stat Hook will feel like a different product category.

## PART 4: ICP INFERENCE FROM ADS

You can reverse-engineer a competitor's Facebook targeting from their ad copy. You don't need access to their ad account. Ads are written for the audience the advertiser is targeting — every word is a signal.

**For each competitor ad, extract:**

```
TARGETING SIGNALS:
- Job title language: [what role is implied? "your team", "your store", "your campaigns"]
- Sophistication level: [novice / intermediate / expert — evident from jargon and assumed knowledge]
- Company size: [solo / SMB / mid-market / enterprise — implied by scale references]
- Industry: [explicit or implied]
- Geography: [from language, currency, case study locations, regional references]
- Trigger event: [what circumstance is the ad addressing? New to platform? Scaling? Problem arising?]

INFERRED FACEBOOK TARGETING:
- Job titles likely targeted: [e.g. "Marketing Manager", "eCommerce Manager", "Founder"]
- Interest categories likely used: [e.g. Shopify, Klaviyo, Meta Ads, eCommerce]
- Behaviour targeting: [Business owners, Online shoppers, Facebook page admins]
- Audience type: [Cold / Lookalike / Retargeting — evident from ad content awareness level]
```

**Why this matters:** The audience no competitor is targeting is your uncrowded market. If every competitor is targeting "Marketing Manager" at mid-market companies, targeting "Operations Manager" or "Head of Growth" at smaller companies may be both less competitive and more convertible.

## PART 5: REGIONAL TARGETING MATRIX

Build this matrix during raw research. Do not guess — only mark confirmed from actual ad evidence (language, currency, case study location, explicit regional mention).

```
MARKET          | Competitor A | Competitor B | Competitor C | Gap?
----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|------
[Region 1]      |     ✅       |     ✅      |     ❌      |  No
[Region 2]      |     ❌       |     ❌      |     ❌      |  YES
[Region 3]      |     ✅       |     ❌      |     ❌      |  Partial
```

**Confirming regional presence from ads:**
- Language of ad copy (Spanish, Portuguese, Arabic, etc.)
- Currency references in pricing
- Named locations in case studies
- Regional product features mentioned
- Facebook Ad Library Country filter — run the same search filtered to specific countries

**A ❌ gap is not automatically an opportunity.** Verify: is the market absent because competitors haven't gotten there, or because there's no demand? Check search volume, app installs, or industry reports for the region before calling it a gap.

## PART 6: THE WHITESPACE ANALYSIS

After completing Parts 1–5, run this synthesis. This is the first moment of interpretation — everything before this was raw collection.

```
COPY PATTERN WHITESPACE:
- Patterns competitors use heavily: [list]
- Patterns competitors rarely/never use: [list]
- Our opportunity: [which pattern is both effective and uncrowded?]

TOV WHITESPACE:
- Where competitors cluster on the spectrum: [positions]
- Uncrowded positions: [where nobody is]
- Our opportunity: [which position fits our brand AND is open?]

FORMAT/STYLE WHITESPACE:
- What formats everyone is running: [list]
- What formats nobody is running: [list]
- Our opportunity: [which format gaps can we fill credibly?]

ICP WHITESPACE:
- Who competitors are targeting: [audience profiles]
- Who competitors are NOT targeting: [uncovered segments]
- Our opportunity: [which underserved segment has real purchase intent?]

REGIONAL WHITESPACE:
- Markets with no competitor presence: [list]
- Markets underserved by quality: [list]
- Our opportunity: [which market can we enter with genuine advantage?]
```

## HOW THIS FEEDS THE PIPELINE

```
1. brand-project-setup-SKILL  →  Establishes OUR brand truth
2. [THIS LIBRARY used here]   →  Maps THEIR competitive reality
3. apify-pain-research        →  Extracts CUSTOMER pain (VOC)
4. icp-persona-engine         →  Synthesises all three into a TARGET
5. hook-creative-generator    →  Turns target + whitespace into hooks
6. fb-paid-media-expert       →  Executes into ad structure
```

**The competitive intelligence phase answers:**
- What messages are already saturated? (Don't repeat them)
- What formats are overused? (Avoid them or weaponise them differently)
- What emotional territory is unclaimed? (Own it)
- Who isn't being talked to? (Target them)

**The VOC phase (apify-pain-research) answers:**
- What do REAL CUSTOMERS say about the pain in their own words?
- What language do they use that no competitor is using in their ads?

These are separate research sources. Neither replaces the other.

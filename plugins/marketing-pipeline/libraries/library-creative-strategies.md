# Library: Creative Strategies
> The strategic groupings layer that sits ABOVE `library-creative-types` (taxonomy). Where library-creative-types tells you the 7 formats × 16 styles available, this library tells you which strategic POSTURE to pick for the campaign, which format×style combinations serve that strategy, and when each strategy wins or fails. Consumed by `creative-strategy-selector` (picks ONE per campaign with rationale), `hook-creative-generator` (writes hooks aligned to the strategy), and `creative-interrogator` (audits whether work serves the declared strategy).

---

## THE STRATEGY VS TAXONOMY DISTINCTION

- **Taxonomy** (`library-creative-types`): "What KIND of ad can I make?" — Static, Video, Carousel × UGC, Founder Story, Pattern Interrupt, etc.
- **Strategy** (THIS library): "What WORKING MECHANIC am I betting this campaign on?" — the underlying behavioural lever the campaign uses to stop the scroll and earn the click.

A campaign picks ONE strategy. That strategy compatible with 2–4 format×style combos from the taxonomy. The strategy is the bet; the format×style is the execution.

---

## THE 6 CORE STRATEGIES

### 1. BEFORE / AFTER

> **The mechanic:** Visual or narrative contrast between two states. Buyer's brain auto-completes the journey from one to the other and projects themselves into it.

**When this wins:**
- Outcome-driven categories where the "after" is visualisable and specific (fitness, finance, productivity, design tools, hair, skin, ecom)
- Themes: TRANSFORMATION, LOSS AVERSION (when "before" = the loss), AUTHORITY (when "after" = expert outcome)
- Personas: identity-driven, social-proof-driven decision styles
- Sophistication Stages 1–3 (still works on plain claim + escalation)

**When this fails:**
- Abstract or intangible outcomes (philosophy products, conceptual SaaS)
- Categories where "before" can't be honestly shown without being uncomfortable for the buyer
- Stage 5 markets — Before/After reads as cliché

**Format×style combos that serve it:**
- Static image — Before/After split layout
- Mid-form video (15–60s) — Before/After narrative arc with mechanism in the middle
- UGC Talking Head — customer tells their before-and-after in their own voice
- Carousel — 4-card before / mechanism / step / after structure

**Hook structures that pair (from library-hook-structures):**
- Pain Agitation → Resolution
- Specific Result Reveal
- Time-bound transformation ("90 days ago vs today")

**The fail mode:** Vague "afters." If the buyer can't imagine the specific new state, the contrast doesn't move them. Avoid stock-photo "happy person at laptop" afters.

### 2. VISUAL PATTERN INTERRUPTER

> **The mechanic:** Deviation from the dominant visual register of the feed. The eye fixates on what doesn't match. Earns attention by being visually wrong-on-purpose for the placement.

**When this wins:**
- Cold acquisition on Meta/TikTok/IG where the feed has collapsed into visual sameness
- Saturated categories (Sophistication Stage 4–5) where the standard category aesthetic has been done to death
- Themes: NOVELTY, CONTRARIAN, IDENTITY (when the deviation signals "we're different on purpose")
- Brands with creative authority — pattern interrupts fail when they look like a desperate brand trying too hard

**When this fails:**
- Risk-averse decision styles — pattern interrupts can read as untrustworthy
- Categories where buyer trust is built through familiarity (financial services, healthcare) — deviation triggers skepticism
- When the deviation isn't backed by a strong second beat — interrupt without payoff is just noise

**Format×style combos that serve it:**
- Static image — Pattern Interrupt style, Typography-as-image, unexpected colour-block
- Short-form video — opening frame visually unexpected (handwritten note, raw camera, broken aspect ratio)
- Carousel — first card is the pattern break, subsequent cards deliver

**Hook structures that pair:**
- Unexpected Object / Frame ("what you're looking at is...")
- Direct Address Disruption ("If you scroll past this, you'll miss...")
- Visual-Word Mismatch (image says one thing, text says the opposite)

**The fail mode:** Random weirdness with no follow-through. The interrupt has to BE about something. Naked pattern breaks without an idea attached produce attention but not action.

---

### 3. CURIOSITY + COGNITIVE HOOKS

> **The mechanic:** Open an information gap the brain can't resist closing. The buyer keeps reading/watching to resolve the cognitive tension. Information theory — Loewenstein's curiosity gap.

**When this wins:**
- Unaware and Problem-Aware audiences (they need to discover the problem AND the solution)
- Categories where the mechanism is the differentiator (Sophistication Stage 3)
- Themes: NOVELTY, AUTHORITY, CONTRARIAN
- Analytical decision styles — they want to learn before they buy

**When this fails:**
- Impulse-purchase categories (curiosity is too slow)
- Buyers who are Solution-Aware or higher (gap-close happens too fast and the ad becomes filler)
- When the payoff doesn't justify the curiosity opened — buyers learn to ignore the brand

**Format×style combos that serve it:**
- Short/mid-form video — open with a question or claim, deliver the answer in the body
- Education / Value-First style — teach something useful, the product is the implied "easier way"
- Talking Head — direct delivery of the gap and the resolution
- Carousel — gap on card 1, payoff progressively through cards

**Hook structures that pair:**
- The Question Hook
- The Specific Number ("X% of marketers don't know...")
- The Counterintuitive Claim
- The "Most People Think X. Actually..." Reframe

**The fail mode:** Clickbait without substance. The gap opens but the payoff is hollow. Burns trust fast.

---

### 4. TRUST & AUTHENTICITY-DRIVEN

> **The mechanic:** Earn the click through credibility signals — real people, real numbers, real names, real artefacts. Bet against the polish-fatigue of the modern feed.

**When this wins:**
- Risk-averse and authority-driven decision styles
- High-consideration purchases (B2B SaaS, enterprise, healthcare, finance, legal, education)
- Themes: AUTHORITY, SOCIAL PROOF, CONTRARIAN, BELONGING
- Categories saturated with overproduced creative (Sophistication Stage 4–5) — authenticity becomes the pattern interrupt

**When this fails:**
- Impulse-purchase aesthetic categories (fashion, lifestyle, beauty) where polish is part of the value
- Buyer identity-driven categories where the brand IS the aspiration — gritty authenticity undercuts it
- When the "authenticity" is performative — buyers can smell a fake-raw founder video instantly

**Format×style combos that serve it:**
- UGC — actual customer footage, phone-shot, sound-on
- Talking Head — founder or expert delivering directly to camera
- Testimonial — full customer narrative with named specifics
- Social Proof / Press — real publications, real names, dated
- Founder Story — direct narrative from the person who built it

**Hook structures that pair:**
- Named Specificity ("Sarah at [recognisable company] said this...")
- Direct Confession ("I built this because [specific problem]...")
- Counter-marketing ("Don't take our word for it. Here's [customer]...")

**The fail mode:** Fake authenticity. Anything that looks like a polished agency's interpretation of "raw" reads as inauthentic and underperforms the polished version it was trying to escape from. Either commit fully or don't bother.

### 5. NARRATIVE & FORMAT HOOK

> **The mechanic:** Open the creative as if it's a story — character, situation, tension, resolution — OR as if it's a different format entirely (a text message, a Slack thread, a news headline, a meme template, an interface screenshot). Earns attention by feeling like content, not advertising.

**When this wins:**
- Mid-form video (15–60s+) where there's time to build narrative arc
- Cold acquisition on TikTok/Reels where native-feeling content outperforms ad-feeling content
- Themes: TRANSFORMATION, IDENTITY, BELONGING, NOVELTY, CONTRARIAN
- Audiences with high ad-skipping behaviour — the format break earns the watch

**When this fails:**
- Compressed placements (story bumpers, 6s pre-rolls) — no room for narrative
- When the brand can't actually deliver narrative craft (badly told stories underperform a clean offer ad)
- Most-Aware audiences — they want the offer, not the story

**Format×style combos that serve it:**
- Short/Mid-form video — narrative arc, character-led
- Founder Story — particularly when there's a real origin story
- UGC — when the customer's story is the narrative
- Static — when the format is the hook (fake text message screenshot, fake interface screenshot, etc.)
- Carousel — narrative beats across cards

**Hook structures that pair:**
- In-Media-Res Story Open ("It was 11pm and I still didn't know which ad...")
- Format Mimicry ("This looks like a Slack thread. It is. From last Tuesday...")
- Character Introduction ("Meet Marcus. He runs marketing at a 50-person SaaS...")

**The fail mode:** Story without point. Narrative that doesn't serve the buying decision is content marketing, not direct response. The story must land on a specific reason to act.

---

### 6. MEME + CULTURED TREND

> **The mechanic:** Borrow the cognitive shortcut of a cultural reference, template, or trending sound. The audience already KNOWS the reference, which compresses meaning and creates instant in-group recognition.

**When this wins:**
- Buyers with high cultural fluency (creator economy, B2C, niche communities, generation-specific products)
- Themes: IDENTITY, BELONGING, CONTRARIAN
- Sophistication Stage 5 — when the category is too tired for claims, memes become the language
- Brands with permission to be playful — established trust + a sense of humour

**When this fails:**
- Brands without permission (enterprise B2B, healthcare, financial services) — using memes reads as cringe
- When the trend is a week past peak (memes have aggressive expiry dates)
- When the reference is misapplied — the audience knows when a meme has been forced
- Authority-driven decision styles — meme aesthetic undermines credibility

**Format×style combos that serve it:**
- Short-form video — trending sound + visual gag
- Static image — meme template adapted to the product
- Lifestyle — when the cultural reference is aesthetic (specific era, specific subculture)
- Pattern Interrupt — when the meme IS the interrupt

**Hook structures that pair:**
- Meme Reference Open (the visual itself is the hook)
- Cultural-In-Joke
- Trend Riding (trending sound or format)

**The fail mode:** Wrong meme, wrong audience, wrong moment. The audience knows when a brand is trying. Forced memes are worse than no memes.

## DECLARATION FORMAT (used by creative-strategy-selector)

```
DECLARED CREATIVE STRATEGY: [name]
RATIONALE: [2-3 sentences — why THIS strategy for THIS persona × THIS theme × THIS sophistication stage]
SOURCE OF DECISION: [TOLD DIRECTLY by user / DERIVED from research — and which research]
COMPATIBLE FORMAT×STYLES (from library-creative-types): [list 2-4]
ALIGNED HOOK STRUCTURES (from library-hook-structures): [list 2-3]
FAIL MODE TO AVOID: [specific to this strategy]
SECONDARY STRATEGY (optional, for variety in same campaign): [name]
```

## DOWNSTREAM SKILL INTEGRATION

This library is consumed by:
- **creative-strategy-selector** — picks ONE strategy + rationale; logs to campaign-state Decision Log
- **hook-creative-generator** — writes hooks aligned to the declared strategy (constrains generation, prevents drift)
- **lp-copy-generator** — LP register matches the strategy (a Trust+Authenticity LP looks different than a Pattern Interrupter LP)
- **cinematic-prompt-architect** — visual prompts honour the strategy's aesthetic
- **creative-interrogator** — audits whether work serves the declared strategy or has drifted
- **library-creative-types** — provides the format×style options; this library constrains which combos to use

---

> **First principle:** Creative strategy is the bet. The format is the execution. Pick the bet first — not the format. Otherwise you make a beautiful ad in a strategy that wasn't going to work, and you can't tell why it failed.
